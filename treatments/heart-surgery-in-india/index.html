Common cardiac procedures

  • Coronary angiography: a test that shows blockages in the heart's arteries.
  • Angioplasty with stent: opening a blocked artery through a catheter.
  • Bypass surgery (CABG): creating new routes for blood flow around blockages.
  • Valve repair or replacement: surgical or, in selected patients, catheter-based (such as TAVI).
  • Pacemaker and other device procedures.

Chest pain, breathlessness at rest or fainting can be an emergency. Call 112 or go to the nearest hospital first. Plan travel only when doctors say it is safe.

Reports to share

  • ECG and echocardiography (echo) reports
  • Angiography report and CD, if already done
  • TMT / stress test, if done
  • Blood tests including kidney function and blood sugar
  • Current medicines, especially blood thinners

How long to plan for

ProcedureTypical planning
AngiographyOften a day-care or short stay; plan 2–4 days in the city
AngioplastyUsually a short hospital stay; plan about a week in the city
Bypass or valve surgeryOften around a week in hospital; plan roughly 2–3 weeks in the city for recovery and the first review

These are general planning guides. Your cardiologist or surgeon will confirm the actual timeline for the patient.

What affects the cost

  • The procedure and whether more than one is needed
  • Number and type of stents, or type of valve
  • Days in ICU and in the ward, and room category
  • Age and other conditions (diabetes, kidney disease)
  • Surgeon and hospital

Common questions about heart surgery & cardiac care

Can I compare bypass surgery estimates from different hospitals?

Yes. With the angiography report and other tests, we request opinions and written estimates from 2–3 accredited hospitals and show them side by side.

How soon can the patient travel home after heart surgery?

Travel is decided by the surgeon, usually after the first review. We plan the stay so that you are close to the hospital until the doctor says it is safe to travel.

Do you arrange travel for a patient who can't walk long distances?

Yes. Tell your coordinator; we arrange wheelchair assistance at stations and airports where available, and suitable cabs.

Is angioplasty always needed after angiography?

No. Treatment depends on what the angiography shows and on the cardiologist's assessment. Options include medicines, angioplasty or bypass surgery.

Do we have to pay Irynt?

No, our help is free. If you want a room, cabs or a helper, we'll tell you the price first. The hospital's estimate is passed on to you exactly as they gave it.

Want us to look at your case?

Tell us what's going on. We'll call you back within 2 hours and take it from there.
