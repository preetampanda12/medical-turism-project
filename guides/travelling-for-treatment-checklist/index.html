What to pack and plan when travelling for treatment

Going to another city for treatment is stressful enough. Use this list so nothing important gets left behind.

Written by the Irynt team · Updated 10 November 2026

Documents

  • All medical reports, scans (with CDs or films) and earlier discharge summaries, in one folder
  • Photo ID for the patient and attendant (Aadhaar or passport)
  • Health insurance card and policy copy, or Ayushman Bharat card
  • Hospital appointment confirmation and the written estimate
  • International patients: passport, visa, hospital letter

Medicines and health

  • Current medicines for the whole trip plus a few extra days, in original packaging
  • A written list of medicines with doses
  • Ask your doctor which medicines (such as blood thinners) need to be stopped before surgery. Never stop on your own.

Money

  • The deposit amount the hospital needs at admission, and how it can be paid
  • A working debit card and UPI; some cash for small expenses

Stay and daily life

  • Stay close to the hospital, with a lift or ground-floor room if mobility is limited
  • Comfortable, loose clothes, slippers with grip, a shawl or jacket for air-conditioned hospitals
  • Phone charger, power bank, and a list of important numbers on paper

On admission day

  1. Reach early, with reports and ID.
  2. Complete admission and insurance desk formalities.
  3. Keep the attendant pass safe.
  4. Note the names of the treating doctor and the ward's contact number.

With Irynt, your coordinator shares a personalised version of this checklist and an assistant can be with you on admission day.

Common questions

How many family members should travel with the patient?

Usually one or two. Hospitals often allow only one attendant at a time in the ward, so more people mean more cost without much more help.

What should we do if the patient's condition worsens while travelling?

Go to the nearest hospital emergency or call 112. Then inform your coordinator and the treating hospital.
