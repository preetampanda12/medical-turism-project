How to choose a hospital for your treatment

The checklist our team uses.

Written by the Irynt team · Updated 10 November 2026

1. Accreditation

NABH (National Accreditation Board for Hospitals & Healthcare Providers) is India's main hospital accreditation; some hospitals also hold JCI (Joint Commission International) accreditation. Accreditation means the hospital meets defined standards for patient safety and quality. It doesn't guarantee an outcome, but it is a strong starting point.

2. The specialist and the team

  • How often does the surgeon or team perform this specific procedure?
  • Is there a team approach (for example, a tumour board for cancer)?
  • Who will look after the patient after surgery and in ICU?

3. The estimate, read carefully

  • Is it written, and what does it include: room, surgeon fees, implants, medicines, ICU days?
  • What is excluded, and what could increase the bill?
  • How long is the estimate valid?

Read our guide to understanding hospital cost estimates.

4. Practical fit for your family

  • Affordable stays nearby for a long treatment
  • Language support
  • Cashless insurance or government scheme empanelment, if you need it
  • Waiting time for the first appointment and for surgery

5. Follow-up

Ask how follow-up will work after you return home: teleconsultation, when to come back, and which tests can be done locally.

Questions to ask before you decide

  1. What exactly is the recommended treatment, and are there alternatives?
  2. How many days in hospital, and how long should we stay in the city?
  3. What does the estimate include and exclude?
  4. What are the main risks for this patient?
  5. How will follow-up work?

Common questions

Is a JCI-accredited hospital always better than an NABH-accredited one?

Not necessarily. Both are meaningful quality accreditations. The specialist's experience with your specific procedure and the practical fit for your family matter just as much.

Should I get more than one opinion?

For major surgery or cancer treatment, a second opinion is common and sensible. We typically share 2–3 hospital opinions for comparison.
